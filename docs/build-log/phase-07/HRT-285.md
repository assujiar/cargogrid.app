## 1. What this checkpoint builds

## 3. Source/config decisions (disclosed, not left implicit)

### Decision 1 — zero migration, zero new RPC (mandatory reading item, taken literally)

Every read/write in this checkpoint is a thin composition over an EXISTING, already-authority-checked RPC. No table was added, no `create or replace function` touches any pre-existing function, and no new `SECURITY DEFINER` boundary was created — the TypeScript composition layer itself performs no authority decision of its own; every decision it surfaces is the owning capability's own.

### Decision 2 — MSS approvals queue composes exactly 4 of 6 candidate domains; attendance-correction and schedule-swap decisions are deliberately excluded, not silently dropped

Read directly against the live migration SQL (not assumed from precedent) and live-confirmed with a real forged-session `psql` probe (§7): `app.list_attendance_correction_requests` (HRT-278) and `app.list_schedule_swap_requests` (HRT-279) both gate on plain `HRS:View` ONLY — neither function's own predicate has a self/direct-manager branch the way `list_overtime_requests`/`list_timesheet_entries`/`list_leave_requests`/`list_schedule_assignments`/`list_performance_outcomes` all independently do. A bare direct manager holding zero extra `HRS` permission gets **zero rows** from either RPC — live-proven, §7. Composing these into the MSS queue would either render permanently empty (dead UI, taxonomy C-20's inverse) or require this checkpoint to invent a NEW manager-scope predicate the owning capability itself never built — forbidden by section 13 ("never re-implement, duplicate, or shadow any of their logic"). Excluded and disclosed here, not silently omitted.

### Decision 3 — the MSS team-id intersection filter is load-bearing, not decorative (the elevated composition-layer leakage risk this prompt was built to guard against)

Every MSS list (`teamScheduleUpcoming`, `teamGoalAssignments`, `teamOutcomes`, `teamTrainingEnrollments`, `teamCertificates`, and every approval-queue item) is filtered a SECOND time in `server/queries/self-service.ts`, client-side in the TS composition layer, against the caller's own `app.list_my_team_employees` roster — even though every owning RPC already scopes a bare caller to self/direct-manager on its own. **Live-proven this second filter is genuinely necessary, not redundant** (§7): an actor holding `HRS:View personal data` (a realistic, ordinary case — many senior-manager roles are ALSO granted broader HR visibility in a real tenant) calling `list_performance_outcomes(employeeId=null)` gets **every** employee's outcome in the tenant, not just their own direct reports'. Without this checkpoint's own defensive re-filter, a dual-role manager visiting the MSS team workspace would see every colleague's performance rating, not just their genuine 2 direct reports' — exactly the inference/leakage shape section 16 names ("a manager's team-summary widget must show only what... the KPI capability's own manager-scope check would separately allow"). The re-filter closes that gap for a dual-role actor without weakening or duplicating the owning capability's own RLS/RPC scope for a plain manager (for whom the two filters agree exactly, also live-confirmed, §7).

### Decision 4 — payroll is entirely absent from the MSS team workspace

`server/queries/self-service.ts`'s `getMssTeamWorkspace` imports nothing from `server/queries/payroll.ts`. Section 15 scopes payroll to ESS "own payslip/benefit" only; section 16's "manager status alone must never grant payroll... data" is satisfied structurally — there is no code path by which a team member's compensation could reach this page.

### Decision 5 — talent-review/pool/succession is entirely absent from MSS reads, and the RPC layer independently enforces this even if it weren't

`app.can_view_talent_review_row`/the pool-membership check are `HRS:Override`-only (HRT-284 decision 6) — never self, never direct manager. This checkpoint's own composition never calls `list_talent_review_cycles`/`list_talent_pools`/`list_succession_candidates` for the team workspace at all; and a live probe (§7) confirms that even if it had, a real direct manager holding zero `HRS:Override` is HARD-REJECTED (`insufficient_authority`) by the RPC itself, not merely returned an empty list — a second, independent line of defense.

### Decision 6 — the composed decide dispatcher is a pure router (business rule 24, taken literally)

`decideManagerApprovalQueueItem` performs zero authority check, zero business validation beyond Zod input-shape/discriminant checking, and writes zero audit row of its own. Every actual authority check, self-decision block, idempotency guard, and audit-log write for every one of the four decision kinds is the owning capability's own already-`VERIFIED`/`COMPLETED` mutation, completely unchanged.

### Decision 7 — deliberately NOT built, disclosed rather than silently dropped (taxonomy C-23 discipline)

No self-service preference/saved-view/acknowledgement schema (decision 1 — genuinely not needed). No REST/GraphQL adapter (repository-wide precedent). No Playwright/axe-core E2E spec (repository-wide precedent, every prior HRT checkpoint's own identical gap). No customer-facing full self-service portal (explicitly Step 13, section 15's own boundary). No native/offline app (explicitly out of scope, section 15). No quick "clock in/out" action duplicated onto the ESS home — `app/(tenant)/[tenantSlug]/hris/my/attendance` already has one (HRT-278); the ESS home links to it rather than re-implementing it, matching this checkpoint's own "never a second... datastore" discipline extended to UI actions too.
